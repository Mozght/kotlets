How to build a trip budget that you will actually use

A useful trip budget is not a single scary number. It is a short list of the costs that actually move: where you sleep, how you get there, what you eat, and what you have already decided to do. This trip budget calculator is built around those four lines, plus an optional “other” line and a buffer. Work through them once before you book, then again after the big tickets are paid, so the remaining figure is what you still have to spend on the ground.

Start with travellers and dates. Lodging almost always follows nights. Food almost always follows days. A Friday-to-Monday city break is three nights in a room and four days of meals. The form fills days with nights + 1 automatically; change it if you arrive late and skip a meal day, or if you want to count only the days you will eat out.

Lodging, transport, food and activities

Put the room or apartment rate in the unit you were actually quoted. “Per night for the group” is the usual hotel or whole-apartment price. “Per person per night” is handy for hostels and some packages. “Total for the trip” is right when you already have a paid booking or a fixed invoice.

Transport is often one or two big tickets (flights, long-distance trains) plus smaller local costs. If you only know the price per person, use that mode and the calculator will multiply by the group. If you have a family ticket or a shared car, use a group total. Keep airport transfers in this line or in “other”, but do not forget them — they are a common hole in first-pass budgets.

Food is the line people under-estimate. A per-person, per-day average that includes coffee, lunch, dinner and a drink is more honest than a restaurant dinner-only figure. If breakfast is in the room rate, lower the daily food amount rather than pretending those meals are free. Activities can be a group total (a tour you will book once) or a per-person figure (museum cards or a class).

Add a buffer, then decide what is prepaid

A 10% buffer is a reasonable default for a first estimate. Increase it when you have not booked yet, when prices are in a currency you do not use every day, or when tips and cash extras are likely. Decrease it when flights and lodging are already paid and you are only planning pocket money.

Once the total looks real, split it mentally in two: prepaid (already on a card, or about to be) and on-the-ground (food, local transport, tickets you will buy there). The prepaid part should sit in the trip total so you know what the holiday really costs. It should not sit in the daily spending cap you take to dinner. Per-person and per-day figures on the results card help you have that conversation with the people you travel with.

The calculator does not fetch exchange rates or hotel prices — it runs in your browser, and the amounts you type stay on your device. Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a trip budget?

Add lodging, transport, food, activities and a little else, then divide by the number of travellers. In this calculator, lodging can be a nightly rate or a trip total, food is usually a per-person daily amount times calendar days (nights + 1), and you can add a percentage buffer for tips, snacks and price changes.

Should I budget by nights or by days?

Use nights for lodging and days for food. A Friday-to-Monday trip is three nights and four days, so you eat more times than you pay for a hotel. The calculator sets days to nights + 1 until you change it.

What percentage should I add as a buffer?

Ten percent is a sensible starting point for a first estimate. Use more if prices are in another currency, if you have not booked yet, or if the destination is heavy on tips and cash extras. Use less if most costs are already prepaid.

Does this trip budget calculator convert currencies?

No. Pick one currency and enter every amount in that currency. If a flight is in euros and lodging is in dollars, convert one of them first using a rate you trust, then enter both here. The Kotlets Travel Planner lets you log each booking in its own currency and convert with your own rates.

Is a prepaid flight part of the daily budget?

Include prepaid flights and lodging in the trip total so you know the real cost, but do not divide them into a daily spending cap. Use the on-the-ground lines — food, local transport, activities — when you decide how much cash or card budget to take each day. You can keep prepaid bookings and day-to-day expenses on separate tabs in the Travel Planner (opens in a new tab).
