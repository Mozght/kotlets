How to work out a monthly vacation savings amount

The reliable way to save for a holiday is to give it a number and a date, then turn that into a transfer you can actually make. This vacation savings calculator does that piece of arithmetic. It takes a target, anything you have already set aside, and the date you want the money ready, and it returns a monthly and a weekly amount. If you already know what you can spare each month, it will also tell you whether that is enough.

Be precise about the target. Some people mean “the remaining cost of a trip I have already partly booked”. Others mean “a pot I want in the bank before I book anything”. Both are valid, but they are different numbers. If flights are already paid, either drop them from the target or count them in “already saved” so you do not save for them twice. Use the trip budget and ski trip cost calculators first if you still need a realistic trip total.

Time left, months and weeks

Time left is the number of days between the start date and the target date. The start date defaults to today; change it to your next payday if the first transfer will not leave the account until then. Months are average calendar months of 365.25 ÷ 12 = 30.4375 days, so a 365-day year is 12 months and a leap year is only a fraction longer. That is easier to plan with than counting month-starts of uneven length. Weekly amounts use the same day count divided by seven.

If the target date is today or in the past, the calculator does not spread the remainder over a fictional month — it tells you the remaining amount is due now. If you have already reached the target, it says so and does not invent a monthly figure.

On-track checks

The optional monthly field is there for a standing order you already have, or for a number you can afford that may not match the “needed” figure. The tool projects a hit date from that amount (using the same average month) and compares it with your deadline. If you would arrive late, you will also see the shortfall on the target date — useful when you are deciding whether to push the trip, raise the transfer, or trim the budget.

A calculator cannot watch the transfers for you. Once you have a monthly amount, the habit that works is a named pot (a sub-account or a line in a budget) and an automatic transfer on payday. Frequently asked questions

How much should I save each month for a vacation?

Subtract what you have already saved from the target, then divide the remainder by the time left until your date. This calculator uses average calendar months (365.25 ÷ 12 = 30.4375 days), so a full year is exactly 12 months, and it also shows a weekly amount.

What if I already know how much I can save each month?

Type that amount in the optional monthly field. The calculator will project the date you would hit the target and say whether that is on track for your deadline. If you are behind, it shows the shortfall on the target date.

Should I include flights I have already paid for?

If the target is “what I still need for the trip”, do not include amounts already paid — put those in “already saved”, or leave them out of the target. If the target is “what the whole holiday will cost”, keep the full figure and count prepaid tickets as already saved.

What date should I use as the start?

Use today if you will make the first transfer now. Use the date of your next payday if that is when the standing order starts. The time left is measured in days between the two calendar dates you enter, so the start date matters.

How is this different from a travel fund in a budget planner?

This page answers “how much, by when”. A travel fund in a household budget also tracks the transfers you actually make, the starting pot, and whether you are still on track as the months go by. The 2027 Budget Planner (opens in a new tab) has a dedicated travel / trip fund for that.
